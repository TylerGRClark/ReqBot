# Splitting long audit questions into a lead and a/b/c parts: test result

*For Tyler. Experiment only: nothing live changed, no checklist, index or question was edited. The numbers below are machine checks. Nobody has yet judged whether a split is faithful or easier; that needs a different, blind reviewer (a ready sheet is included).*

## The short version

- On five documents the model was asked about **197 long or stacked questions**: a random sample of at most 40 per document, out of 378 such questions and 763 questions in all.
- It said **keep (already one thing) for 67** (34%) and proposed a split for **130**.
- Of those 130 splits, **80 (62%) passed all seven machine checks**; the other 50 fell back to today's question unchanged. **The target was 80%, so it was missed.**
- **No row was dropped** (197 in, 197 out); every question that failed a check simply stays as it is today.
- The longest piece got shorter: among the 80 changed rows the median longest piece went from 28 to 19 words (90th percentile 37 to 30). Across all 197 candidates, counting the keeps and the fallbacks at their old length, the median went from 28 to 24 and the 90th percentile from 38 to 36.
- **A third of the passing splits are not really easier.** In 25 of the 80 the longest piece is still more than 80% as long as today's question; in 11 the model added 'as appropriate' / 'as required' / 'when applicable', which is in neither the question nor the source and makes a duty sound optional; in 3 the lead question stops mid-sentence. Only 44 of the 80 are free of all three.
- **My recommendation: do not ship this as it is.** The idea works on clean lists (see the best rows below), but the machine checks are not enough, and the model copied a hedge phrase from my own prompt example. A second, tighter round and a blind review of the rows below would be the next step; nothing needs deciding before 2026-10-15.

## How the test was run

- Two tuning rounds on three other documents (AFI 10-2402, AFI 17-203, DAFMAN 17-1305), then the wording was frozen and the five test documents were run once.
- Tuning: round 1 passed 38 of 90 proposed splits (42%); round 2 passed 65 of 87 (75%). The big win in round 2 was telling the model to repeat the noun in each part instead of writing 'they' or 'it'.
- The test result fell short of tuning (61% vs 75%), as expected for wording tuned on three documents and tried on five new ones. DODI 5200.48 was the weakest (10 changed out of 22 proposed), and the model often produced more than five parts.
- About 440 model calls in all, on the local model that writes today's questions. The model was not run twice on the same row, so run-to-run noise is not measured.

## What still looks wrong

1. **The model invents hedges.** In 11 passing splits a part carries 'as appropriate', 'as required' or 'when applicable' that the source does not have. My worked example in the prompt used 'as appropriate', and the model copied it. A hedge on a duty is the very thing you were wary of (a duty turning into something optional). The machine checks only looked for dropped qualifiers, not added ones.
2. **Not shorter.** 25 of 80 passing splits repeat the whole question as the lead, or repeat a long closing condition in every part, so the auditor reads the same long words several times. Many 'in accordance with X, Y, and Z' rows came out this way; for those, the split is arguably worse than today's single question.
3. **Lists that are not independent.** Where the question mixes verbs and objects (rows #38 and #50) the model splits them in ways the source does not say. The checks cannot see this; only a reader can.
4. **Leads can be empty.** 'Does the unit do all of the following?' is formulaic and tells the auditor nothing alone. It is safe, but it is not the 'short lead question' you described.
5. **Broken sentences.** Three leads end mid-sentence ('... to ensure?', '... according to?'). One part turns 'may be omitted' into 'is omitted' (row #58).
6. **Wording drift.** Some parts reword the source ('followed' for 'in accordance with', 'exist' for 'provide'). Each is small; together they are the 'different' you said a rewrite must never be.
7. **Checks are strict in places and blind in others.** They are strict on synonyms, 'within' for places, and on sentence-initial capitals; they are blind to a verb moved to the wrong object, an added hedge, or a lead that is just filler.
8. **Only 197 of 378 candidates were tried** (a sample of at most 40 per document), and only one model at one setting.
9. **A fault that is already in today's question** (not caused by the split): for DODI 8551.01 paragraph 1.1 the source says this issuance 'Does not alter or override' existing authorities; today's question reads 'Does this issuance alter or override ...?', which asks the opposite. The split inherited that (it was sent back for another reason). It is the kind of dropped 'not' you were worried about, found in the current questions.
