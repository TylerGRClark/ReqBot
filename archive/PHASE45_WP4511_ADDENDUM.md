# WP-45.11 addendum — T2 is tested on the chunks it changes, not by re-running the whole corpus (written before any T2 run)

*Follows [PHASE45_WP4511_PLAN.md](PHASE45_WP4511_PLAN.md) (#247). New information since that plan: the table fix (T2, #249) turned out to be narrow. The first versions resolved every generic chunk item and changed many chunks; review showed that dropped list markers (chunks opening with a marker 41 to 24) and restored text that today's code silently drops. The final version swaps only the flat block of a table for its grid and leaves everything else byte-for-byte as the chunker wrote it. No T2 arm has run.*

## 1. What changed in the evidence

Verified on all 1,166 chunks of the 13 pinned documents at the 256-token default (old function from `main` against the new one): **exactly the 9 chunks that hold a generic table change; the other 1,157 are byte-identical. Of those 9, only 5 reach Step C** (DODI 5200.48 chunks 76 and 87, DODI 8551.01 chunk 26, afi10-2402 chunks 121 and 123); the other 4 sit in a skipped glossary-type section (3) or the table of contents (1) and are dropped by the pipeline's filters in both versions. Today's flat-table problem therefore reaches the extraction model in 5 chunks of 839. The chunk count stays 839 (no renumbering), chunks opening with a list marker stay 41 and marker lines 167, chunks carrying a grid go 11 to 16.

Consequence for the design: a full re-run of the corpus for T2 would feed Step C the **same text** for 830 of 839 chunks. Whatever differs in the output of those chunks is noise (the 4 changed chunks that the filters drop do not reach Step C either), and it is exactly the noise B0a/B0b already measured (recall 45 against 50 of 74, quote-set overlap 0.85). Re-running them cannot say anything about T2 and would bury its signal. The plan's recall-based rules R1 and R2 therefore hold **by construction** for T2 (identical inputs), which is shown, not assumed, by the byte comparison above (re-run as part of the T2 report).

## 2. The T2 test (replaces the plan's full-corpus T2 arm; T1 and T3 are unchanged)

- **Base.** T2 is layered on the accepted T1 state (plan section 2). If T1 is adopted, the byte comparison, the filtering and the subset below are **recomputed from the Docling 2.122.0 chunk files** (`default` against `T2_default`), because the upgrade changes the table count and cell text and the affected set need not be the same five; if T1 is not adopted, the 2.94.0 files are used as described here.
- **Inputs.** The 5 changed chunks that reach Step C, as chunk files holding only those records per document (chunk ids unchanged between the two versions): `T2sub_base` (today's text) and `T2sub_new` (T2's text). Same model, prompt, Step D and parameters, run by `run_arm.py` from a pinned worktree.
- **Repeats.** Each version is run **5 times** (`T2base_1..5`, `T2new_1..5`), interleaved base/new so server drift cannot favor one side. Step C samples at temperature 0.1, so repeats are the noise estimate for each chunk.
- **Measures, per chunk.** (a) Step C records and Step D survivors per repeat, with their range; (b) the set of distinct normalized quotes each version yields and how often each quote appears across its 5 repeats; (c) for each table in the 9 chunks, whether any quote is a grid row or cell content, and whether it is grounded in the chunk text (Step D's own `quote_not_grounded_in_chunk` count); (d) **every record from these chunks, from all 10 runs, read by me (5 chunks, so a few dozen records per run)** and summarized per chunk.
- **Decision rules (fixed now).** T2 is *adopted for a production pull request* only if: **R2a** no chunk's median survivor count falls by more than 2 against today's text, and none falls to zero where today's text gave records; **R3** at least one of the 5 chunks gives, in at least 3 of 5 repeats, a quote that is a grid row or cell of its table where today's text gave none in 3 of 5, and **no quote of any kind** (table, prose or list) that appears in at least 3 of 5 repeats of today's text and is grounded in the chunk disappears from all 5 repeats of the new text without an explanation I read (changing the table's form changes the model's input for the whole chunk, so a loss of an ordinary requirement in the same chunk counts exactly like a loss of a table row); **R4** the read of all records finds no new systematic defect (for example quotes that merge two cells). A chunk whose new-text quotes are less often grounded is listed and read.
- **What this cannot show.** Five chunks in a handful of tables cannot show an effect on overall recall; it can show whether the grid helps or hurts extraction from tables like these, and that nothing else moved. If the 9 chunks give nothing readable either way, T2 is reported as *no demonstrated difference*; the argument for it then rests on the grid being the format WP-42 chose on purpose and on correctness (the quote and the table's layout), which is the owner's call.

## 3. Consequence for T3

T3 (the chunk limit) is still tested as a full-corpus arm, on the T2 state, because every chunk changes there. T3's chunk files were regenerated from the final T2 code at 512 and 1,024 tokens. At 1,024, grid-carrying chunks go from 0 (today's code) to 14 with T2. T3 needs two replicates of the T2 state as its noise floor (T2a, T2b: full runs of the final T2 chunk files at 256 tokens, which differ from B0 in only the 9 chunks), as the plan says; those two runs also serve as a second, larger measure of noise on identical inputs.
